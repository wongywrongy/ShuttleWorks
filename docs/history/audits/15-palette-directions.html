Three ways out of the default palette

The token architecture is sound — two layers, an elevation ladder, a contrast gate. The values are the problem: #2563EB is Tailwind blue-600 over a stock cool-gray ramp, so the product reads as a competent shadcn app rather than as ShuttleWorks. Each direction below is a mapping change, not a component sweep. Pick one; A and C combine.

Same specimen, every time A court strip, the status vocabulary, a primary action, and a sign-in field — the objects that actually carry the product.
Both themes, side by side Light and dark are two mappings of one semantic layer, so a direction has to survive both or it isn't a direction.

A

Warm the substrate, keep the role

Ten primitive values. The cheapest way to stop looking generated.
#0F62B8 accent #FAF8F5 base #17140F ink #8C8477 control

The neutral ramp moves off cool-220 to a low-saturation warm — a hall floor rather than a screenshot — and the accent moves off blue-600 to a deeper, cyan-leaning azure that holds its own against the warm ground.

Deliberately a whisper of warmth, not a cream page: warm-cream-plus-serif is its own cliché and this is an operations tool, not a magazine.

RiskLowest. Blue still means both scheduled and clickable — this direction does not fix the collision.

B

Petrol accent, blue retired

Fewer hues, each meaning exactly one thing.
#0A6B75 accent #F7F9F8 base #2E7D3C live #56635E scheduled

The accent becomes the petrol of a court surround, and blue leaves the system entirely: scheduled is demoted to neutral — it is the default state, not an alert — so the only coloured hues left are live, called, blocked, and the one thing you can click.

Live green is pushed warmer (#2E7D3C) so it separates cleanly from the teal accent at chip size.

RiskMedium. Teal and green are 40° apart; the warmed green is what keeps them legible, and that has to hold at 10px on a hall projector. Moves colour on the live Ops board.

C

Keep the palette; spend the identity on type

Colour is not the only axis, and it is the riskiest one to move.
unchanged unchanged unchanged

Geist + JetBrains Mono is itself a default pairing. A condensed grotesque — Archivo Narrow or Barlow Condensed — for match identity, court numbers, and timers borrows from the object every player in the hall is already reading: the scoreboard. Body copy stays Geist.

The signature is the court strip carrying a true-proportion court plan — 13.4 × 6.1 m, tramlines, net, short service lines, the centre-line T — instead of a generic status stripe. It inherits the row's state colour, so a live court lights up.

RiskMedium, none of it colour. The specimen approximates the condensed face with the system stack; the real faces need bundling and a pass over every dense table.

C·1

The court mark, drawn properly

First pass was a vertical bar with a tick through it — not a court.

A badminton court is 13.4 × 6.1 m — a 2.2 : 1 landscape rectangle. The first attempt was a narrow vertical rail, so the proportion was inverted and only one marking was present. Nothing about it read as a court.

This one is drawn from the real markings at true scale: outer boundary, the doubles tramlines 0.46 m in, the net across the middle, the short service lines 1.98 m either side, and the centre lines running back from them — the T that only badminton has.

The doubles long service line (0.76 m from the back) is deliberately omitted: at row size it lands under a quarter of a pixel and only muddies the mark.

OpenThe mark carries state colour. Whether a whole board of lit courts is useful or noisy is a call to make against real fixture data, not a specimen.

Decided: A + C

A and C ship; B is not taken. A costs about ten primitive values and no component changes, and it removes the "generated" read on its own. C is where the identity actually comes from, and it carries zero colour risk, so it runs alongside. B stays on the shelf — its argument is the strongest (one hue means touchable, three mean state, everything else is neutral) but it repaints a surface directors read under time pressure.

What A + C does not fix: blue still means both scheduled and clickable, and --module-meet is still the accent hex. That collision is logged in the debt log and needs its own decision — the cheap half of B (demote --status-started to neutral, leave the accent azure) would close it without repainting anything else.

Whichever wins: it lands as a mapping change in tokens.css, runs through scripts/check-contrast.mjs, and updates DESIGN_COLOR.md. No component sweep — that is what the two-layer system bought.
